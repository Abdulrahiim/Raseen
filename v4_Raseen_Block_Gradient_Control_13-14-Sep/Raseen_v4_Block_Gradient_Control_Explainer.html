Raseen — Block Gradient Control

رَصين — composed, steady, unshaken: the plant that keeps its composure when the cloud comes.

A 3,000 MW solar plant is not one generator. It is thirty 100 MW blocks spread over eight kilometres, and a cloud crosses them one after another in ten minutes. Raseen takes the operator's existing weather forecast as an input, works out when the shadow reaches each block, and drives the blocks' set-points through the existing plant controller so that the grid sees a smooth, pre-declared ramp instead of a 1,800 MW cliff — with no battery, no new hardware, and every instruction already defined in the Saudi Arabian Grid Code.

What changed from v3

Two corrections that make the idea stronger, not weaker

v3 forecast the cloud and pre-positioned a 500 MW battery. Both parts are gone. What is left is smaller, cheaper, and harder to argue with.

Correction 1

The forecast is an input, not our product

Large operators already buy hours-ahead forecasts (satellite, NWP, machine learning) and already run digital twins. Raseen does not compete with that and never says operators are "blind to the weather". The provider says a band arrives between 13:40 and 14:10. It cannot say which of 3,000 inverters loses power at 13:52:30, and it does not decide what the plant should do about it. That conversion — from a forecast to a physically executed, grid-visible response — is our job.

We do not compete with weather forecasting systems. We convert weather forecasts into an optimal physical control response inside the solar plant.

Correction 2

No battery — the flexibility is already inside the plant

A utility-scale battery is the most expensive way to shape a ramp. A gigawatt PV plant already contains everything needed: 3,000 inverters with individual set-points, a Power Plant Controller (PPC), SCADA, 19 weather stations, and 60 km² of geography over which a cloud is never everywhere at once. The energy that shapes the ramp is a thin slice of sunshine deliberately not exported for a few minutes.

Software + the existing PPC and inverters + selected sensors + the digital twin + optimisation. Capital cost: near zero. Operating cost: about one per cent of a day's energy on the day a front crosses.

Three names, one mechanism

Block Gradient Control
The controller. Every 10 s it places the plant's required curtailment on individual blocks by cloud-arrival order, so the plant shows a spatial power gradient — blocks about to be shaded already down, blocks far from the front still up — while the export follows a declared line.
Rolling Solar Reserve
The headroom on blocks the cloud will not reach within the next few minutes. It moves with the front ("moving headroom") and it is the only reserve a PV plant can honestly declare, because headroom on a block expires the moment the shadow arrives.
Dynamic Solar Headroom
The standing version: the Grid Code's Active Power Delta Regulation made dynamic (held only while an event is likely) and spatial (held on the blocks least likely to be shaded). It absorbs partial clouds entirely and lets the plant respond upward to frequency.

How to read it. With Block Gradient Control, the blocks nearest the front are brought down to their post-shading level before the shadow arrives, so nothing steps when it does; the reserve slice Δ sits on the far blocks and rolls east as their ETAs shrink; the export follows the dashed declared line. With a plant-level set-point the export is identical and the energy not exported is identical — but the PPC's proportional rule curtails the blocks that are already shaded, every shading event re-scales all thirty set-points, and the "reserve" is wherever the rule happened to leave it. That difference is the whole idea.

Physics

Five relations the whole idea rests on

Symbols in MW, minutes and MWh. Block i has available active power Ai(t) — what the sun would let it export right now, the Grid Code's own term — and export set-point Pi(t). Plant available A = ΣAi, plant export P = ΣPi.

1 · No storage

Export can never exceed available: P(t) ≤ A(t). Everything the plant does is subtraction. The only question is where and when to subtract.

2 · Headroom expires

hi = Ai − Pi ≥ 0. Headroom on a block exists only while the sun is on it. Headroom is upward reserve with an expiry time equal to the block's cloud arrival time. This is why a plant-level number cannot be a reserve: it does not know which part of itself expires next.

3 · The lead-time contract

For a front of depth D that crosses the plant in τ minutes, holding the export to a down-gradient g requires starting the descent L minutes before fence contact:

L = D/g − τ  ⇔  g = D / (τ + L)

With D = 1,800 MW and τ = 10 min: an upwind mast 8 km out (10 min at 48 km/h) supports 90 MW/min (3 %/min); a 20-minute provider nowcast supports 60 MW/min (2 %/min); no lead at all leaves the natural 180 MW/min. The provider supplies L; Raseen turns L into a guaranteed g.

4 · The energy cost is set by the gradient, not by the allocation

Spilled energy on the way down, for a linear front with natural rate r = D/τ: Edown = (r − g) · τ · (D/g) / 2. For the design front: 30 MWh at 150 MW/min, 75 at 120, 150 at 90, 300 at 60. Any allocation of the curtailment among blocks spills exactly the same energy. BGC does not spill less than a plant-level controller for a perfectly forecast front — we say so before the jury does. What it adds is listed below.

5 · Rolling Solar Reserve

The reserve the plant can honestly promise for the next Hr minutes is the headroom on blocks the cloud will not reach within Hr: R(t, Hr) = Σ { hi : ETAi > Hr }. It rolls because blocks drop out of the set as the front advances and new headroom is placed further along its path. R is the number that goes to the TSO as firm upward reserve.

Before · during · after

The controller works in all three phases — and the exit needs no forecast

Frames from the design case (Block Gradient Control, g = 90 MW/min, Δ = 150 MW), rendered by the same model as above. One row of ten blocks; the cloud moves left to right.

Before contact · t = −6 min

The provider's forecast plus the on-site nowcast give the lead L. Descent starts at t = −L, nearest blocks first. The Ramp Event Notice goes to the TSO with the declared line and the firm reserve. If the front stalls, curtailment is released in the same order it was placed — nearest first — and the plant is back at full output within the slew limit.

During transit · t = +2 min

The first blocks touched confirm speed, heading and depth; every downstream ETA is corrected. Shaded blocks sit at their available power and take the reactive duty. The descent continues on the not-yet-shaded blocks so the export stays on the declared line; the reserve slice rolls east one block at a time.

Exit · t = +54 min

The trailing edge frees the western blocks first. Left alone the plant would recover at 180 MW/min. BGC holds the recovering blocks to the declared up-gradient — the headroom wave now travels behind the cloud — and releases the last curtailment when the plant is back at full output. No forecast is involved: curtailment can only remove power, and the blocks recover in the order the sentinels see.

Dynamic Solar Headroom

The standing reserve — Delta Regulation made dynamic and spatial

The Grid Code already defines the mode: Active Power Delta Regulation constrains output to a constant margin below Available Active Power (SAGC definitions; instructable under 2.11.13.11 and 5.4.2.3). Raseen makes Δ dynamic — non-zero only when the forecast says an event is likely, sized to the expected partial-cover loss — and spatial — held on the blocks least likely to be shaded.

Partial clouds become invisible

A two-column-wide band would take 360 MW out at 180 MW/min, twice. With a flat hold, Raseen pre-descends the plant to the transit minimum at 2 %/min, holds 2,640 MW while the band crosses, and re-ascends behind it: 46 MWh not exported, POI ramp 60 MW/min instead of 180, and no dip at all during transit. Try it in the model: Thin band + hold flat.

Upward frequency response becomes possible

2.11.13.10 requires PV plants to modulate active power below 59.8 Hz, but "output power shall be limited by the available output power". At maximum power point a plant can only respond downward. With Δ held, it can respond upward: DSH is what makes a PV plant a bidirectional frequency-response provider when the TSP activates Frequency Regulation (2.11.13.9).

A firm number for the TSO

R(t, Hr) with Δ placed on far-ETA blocks is declarable as reserve for a stated horizon — the basis of a System Service under 2.14.1 "(ix) reduction or increase in Active Power … (xi) any other System Service". Cost: Δ × holding time. 250 MW for 30 minutes = 125 MWh ≈ 0.5 % of a clear day; the optimiser weighs this against the event-triggered alternative every minute.

Honesty box — say it before the jury does

Block Gradient Control does

  • compute the feasible declared trajectory from block-level ETAs and depths;
  • place curtailment so no block steps when the shadow arrives, and change set-points only where the front is;
  • hold a reserve that is firm for a stated horizon, and declare it;
  • release false alarms block by block, nearest first, without a plant-wide re-scale;
  • move reactive duty to shaded inverters, whose apparent-power rating is free;
  • use the same block grid as the sensor that measures the front (the first 20 % of blocks hit give speed, heading and depth for the other 80 %).

It does not

  • spill less energy than a plant-level pre-ramp for a perfectly forecast front — relation 4;
  • stop the plant losing 1,800 MW under full cover — without storage, export ≤ available; the reserve ladder covers the plateau, as the Code intends;
  • forecast the weather — the provider's forecast is an input; Raseen forecasts impact;
  • control anything outside the plant — no loads, no instructions to the TSO, no simulated ride-through.

Architecture

Eight modules, three of them new, one of them the core

Time-domain separation is the principle: milliseconds belong to inverter firmware (ride-through, dynamic voltage support, RoCoF), seconds to the PPC's feedback and droop, 10 seconds to 15 minutes to Raseen, hours to day-ahead to forecast ingestion, planning and declarations.

The allocation, in twelve lines

input:  Aᵢ, Aᵢ⁻ = Aᵢ(1−dᵢ), ETAᵢ, P*(t), Δ, Hʳ, σ, slew, Pᵢ(prev)
C ← max(0, Σ Aᵢ − P*)                          # required curtailment
far ← { i : ETAᵢ > Hʳ and Aᵢ > Aᵢ⁻ }            # reserve carriers
curᵢ ← min(Δ/|far|, Aᵢ − Aᵢ⁻)  for i in far    # 1. reserve slice
rem ← C − Σ curᵢ
wᵢ ← exp(−max(ETAᵢ,0)/σ) for blocks with room   # 2. descend-first, water-filling with caps
while rem > ε and candidates:
    give each candidate share = rem·wᵢ/Σw, capped at (Aᵢ − Aᵢ⁻ − curᵢ); drop capped blocks
Pᵢ* ← Aᵢ − curᵢ                                 # 3. set-points
Pᵢ* ← clamp(Pᵢ*, Pᵢ(prev) ± slew·Δt, ≤ Aᵢ)      # 4. slew limit, then rebalance so Σ Pᵢ* = P*
Qᵢ* ← plant Q order allocated ∝ √(Sᵢ² − Pᵢ*²)     # 5. reactive duty to the freest inverters
output Pᵢ*, Qᵢ* → PPC groups

The same problem is a 30-variable linear programme (minimise Σ wi(Ai − Pi) subject to ΣPi = P*, Ai− ≤ Pi ≤ Ai, slew bounds); it solves in milliseconds. The model on this page runs the heuristic above.

Innovation claims

Exactly what is new — and what is not

Not new — cite it, do not claim it

  • Curtailment-based ramp-rate control without storage, using short-term forecasts, at plant level: demonstrated and quantified (Marcos et al., UPNA, 38.5 MW Amareleja; DLR/Eye2Sky, EPJ Photovoltaics 2024, 20.8 MWp with 13-camera nowcasts, 81 % fewer violations).
  • Headroom-based grid services from a PV plant: NREL / CAISO / First Solar 2017, a 300 MW plant run 30 MW below peak to follow AGC and provide frequency response.
  • Ramp-rate limiting with uniform commands to PV subsystems: patented (GE, US 8,901,411) — reactive, no forecasting, no spatial allocation.

New — say it, and say "to our knowledge"

  1. Block Gradient Control — curtailment allocated across a gigawatt plant by per-block cloud-arrival time, with a designed rolling reserve on the blocks the cloud reaches last, slew-limited feed-forward set-points and reactive-duty migration. Prior work treats the plant as a single unit.
  2. The lead-time contract g = D/(τ + L) as the explicit interface between an external forecast and the plant's guaranteed gradient: the provider supplies L, the plant converts it into g, the notice carries both.
  3. A declarable Rolling Solar Reserve R(t, Hr) — headroom counted only on blocks still in the sun for the stated horizon — mapped to Delta Regulation, updated Declarations and 4.46 notifications, and to the continuous-monitoring compliance path.
  4. Pre-commissioning delivery — the controller is built and exercised on the twin with real irradiance statistics (K.A.CARE RRAtlas, 1-minute data) before the plant exists, so it connects with its ramp behaviour already declared.

Jury questions and the answers that hold

"Operators already forecast clouds."
Yes, and we use their forecast. Forecasting tells you a band arrives at 13:52; it does not tell 3,000 inverters what to do at 13:52:30. We convert the forecast into set-points, block by block, with a declared gradient and a firm reserve.
"Why not just curtail the whole plant with the PPC?"
For a perfectly forecast front you spill the same energy — we say so. A plant-level set-point cannot tell you which headroom expires in three minutes, so it cannot declare a firm reserve; it re-scales all thirty blocks every time one is shaded; it curtails shaded blocks it should leave alone; and it cannot move reactive duty. Block control is what makes the promise executable and declarable.
"You are throwing away energy."
About 1–2 % of annual energy at 3 %/min, measured at real plants; ≈ SAR 7–8 m/yr for this plant. A battery block that shapes a third of the same event costs about SAR 120 m/yr. Spilling the world's cheapest sunshine is the cheapest reserve in the Kingdom. Batteries belong on the evening peak.
"What if the forecast is wrong?"
A late front costs the pre-ramp energy; the table shows it: 24 MWh at 4 %/min, 198 MWh at 2 %/min. We trigger on confidence, release nearest blocks first the moment the sentinels disagree, and report the false-alarm rate as a KPI.
"What if the front is deeper than forecast?"
That is what the reserve slice on the far blocks is for; its size grows with forecast uncertainty. If it is exhausted, the export leaves the declared line — and the notice already told the TSO the residual under full cover.
"Can inverters and the PPC do this? Is 10 seconds fast enough?"
Group active-power limits and reactive set-points are standard PPC functions over Modbus or IEC 61850; we write them every 10 s within slew limits. The shadow crosses a 100 MW block in about a minute, so we move set-points six times per block crossing and the PPC's own loop corrects the residual. Everything faster than a second stays in the firmware the Grid Code certifies; the Code's set-point accuracy test allows 10 minutes.
"Where does the 3 %/min come from?"
From the lead time: 1,800 MW over 10 minutes of crossing plus 10 minutes of lead is 90 MW/min. Give us 20 minutes of lead and it is 60. The number is the provider's lead time turned into a promise.
"Who pays for the curtailment?"
If the TSP instructs a Gradient or Delta, it is settled under the PPA. As a System Service under 2.14.1 it is paid. Self-initiated, it is our cost — about SAR 15,000 per severe event.
"Why inside one plant and not across the region's plants?"
Because that is where the jurisdiction ends. Cross-plant coordination is the TSP's job; each plant declares its own gradient and reserve and National Grid SA schedules the sum. Raseen makes each plant's declaration trustworthy — exactly the input that system-level coordination needs.
"Is the plant real?"
NAJM-3000 is a representative 3 GW park modelled on the current NREP round. The irradiance driving it is real — K.A.CARE 1-minute measurements — and so are the physics engine and the controller; the plant itself is a stand-in until a developer gives us theirs.

الاسم. رَصين: رابط الجأش، ثابت لا يتزعزع — المحطة التي تحفظ رباطة جأشها حين تأتي السحابة. الجملة الأساسية. لا نتنبأ بالطقس؛ نتنبأ بأثره التشغيلي ونتحكم بالمحطة كتلةً كتلةً، فترى الشبكة جدولاً بدل هاوية.

الفكرة. محطة 3,000 ميجاواط ليست مولّداً واحداً؛ هي ثلاثون كتلة بقدرة 100 ميجاواط ممتدة على ثمانية كيلومترات، تعبرها السحابة كتلةً بعد كتلة خلال عشر دقائق. يأخذ رَصين توقعات الطقس التي يمتلكها المشغّل أصلاً كمُدخل — فالتنبؤ بالطقس ليس ابتكارنا — ويحسب لكل كتلة زمن وصول الظل ومغادرته، ثم يقود نقاط ضبط القدرة الفاعلة لكل كتلة عبر متحكم المحطة القائم بحيث ترى الشبكة منحدراً مجدولاً ومُعلَناً بدل هاوية 1,800 ميجاواط — من دون بطارية ولا أجهزة جديدة، وكل تعليمة موجودة أصلاً في الكود السعودي للشبكة.

التحكم بالتدرّج الكتلي. قبل وصول الجبهة تُخفَّض الكتل الأقرب إليها إلى مستواها بعد التظليل فلا تقفز عند وصول الظل؛ ويُحتفظ بشريحة احتياطي على الكتل البعيدة تتدحرج شرقاً مع الجبهة («الاحتياطي الشمسي المتدحرج»)؛ وأثناء العبور تؤكد الكتل الأولى سرعة الجبهة واتجاهها وعمقها فتُصحَّح أزمنة الوصول لبقية الكتل؛ وبعد خروج السحابة تُحجَز الكتل العائدة إلى الشمس عند التدرّج المُعلَن حتى تعود المحطة إلى كامل إنتاجها — من دون أي اعتماد على التوقع في مرحلة الخروج.

هامش الطاقة الشمسية الديناميكي. هو «تنظيم دلتا» في الكود مُحوَّلاً إلى نمط ديناميكي (يُفعَّل فقط حين يكون الحدث محتملاً) ومكاني (على الكتل الأقل عرضة للتظليل). يمتصّ السحب الجزئية كلياً — شريط بعرض عمودين يُخفي هبوطاً بمقدار 360 ميجاواط مقابل 46 ميجاواط-ساعة — ويجعل المحطة قادرة على الاستجابة الترددية صعوداً.

الأرقام. هاوية 1,800 ميجاواط تُسلَّم كمنحدر بـ900 ميجاواط بمعدل 3٪/دقيقة مع إشعار لا يقل عن عشر دقائق مقابل 305 ميجاواط-ساعة (نحو 1.2٪ من طاقة اليوم ≈ 15,000 ريال). وحدة بطارية بقدرة 500 ميجاواط تكلف نحو 1.09 مليار ريال وتُشكّل أقل من ثلث الحدث نفسه. المعادلة الحاكمة: g = D/(τ + L) — مزوّد التوقعات يعطينا زمن الاستباق L، ونحن نحوّله إلى تدرّج مضمون g.

ما لا نفعله. لا نتنبأ بالطقس، لا نتحكم بالأحمال، لا نوجّه تعليمات لمشغل النقل، لا نحاكي تجاوز الأعطال، ولا نحتاج تخزيناً. ولا نقول إن التحكم الكتلي يوفّر طاقة أكثر من التحكم على مستوى المحطة لجبهة متوقَّعة تماماً — الطاقة المُهدرة يحددها التدرّج وزمن الاستباق؛ ما نضيفه هو احتياطي مؤكَّد قابل للإعلان، ودقة تنفيذ بلا قفزات، ونقل واجب القدرة غير الفاعلة، والاستشعار بالكتل نفسها.
