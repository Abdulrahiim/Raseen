3.2 What the Challenge 2 card says — and what it implies

The challenge page gives the problem statement, a "reality today" paragraph, and four "opportunity to impact" targets: raise grid reliability; improve absorption of diverse generation sources; efficiently manage variable loads; guarantee quality and continuity of supply. The downloadable "supporting model for the participant" (نموذج داعم للمشارك) then lists the "current methods" — this list is the closest thing to a rubric you will get, because a solution that visibly improves on one of these is what the challenge author is waiting for:

- Inverters equipped to support the grid with **active and reactive power** in order to comply with the **Electricity Code** (الكود الكهربائي).

- Energy storage systems that **interact with grid variables** and deliver active or reactive power as the grid needs, again for code compliance.

- Technical linking of generation systems to support **optimal and most economical operational planning** of generating stations.

- Use of energy storage systems (listed a second time — a strong hint).

- Smart-grid and **AI applications to forecast electricity production and demand in real time**, supporting grid stability.

- Demand management — encouraging users to defer or reduce consumption.

The supporting files tell you what the author was reading: the IEA report on seasonal and inter-annual variability, SERA's Generation Expansion Planning Criteria (the thumbnail on the card) via the Codes & Standards page, the MDPI paper on deep-reinforcement-learning scheduling of multi-source storage, and the national datasets on consumption, consumption by operational region and peak load, plus GASTAT, Ministry of Energy and SERA open data. The card also lists an item called "Preliminary Statistical Analysis of Variability in Renewable Energy Production – Case Study"; its hyperlink resolves to the open-data datasets rather than to a separate paper, which reads as an invitation to do that analysis yourselves — a variability statistics slide built from RRAtlas irradiance would answer it directly.

4.3 Saudi Arabian Grid Code — the compliance envelope for a Power Park Module

The requirements below were read from the September 2021 edition of the Code (a mirror hosted by ESMAP); the SERA page lists a 1 January 2022 edition and SEC hosts a May 2024 update. Treat them as substantially current but re-check every clause against the 2024 text before you put it on a slide.

| **Requirement**                               | **Value in the Code**                                                                                                                                              |
|-----------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| System frequency                              | 60 Hz. Normal band 59.9–60.1 Hz; continuous operation 58.8–60.5 Hz; 30-minute withstand 57.5–58.7 and 60.6–61.5 Hz; 30-second withstand 57.0–57.4 and 61.6–62.5 Hz |
| Rate of change of frequency                   | Ride through 2.5 Hz/s (500 ms window)                                                                                                                              |
| Frequency response (droop)                    | 2–8% adjustable, 4% typical, in both directions; activated/deactivated on TSP request                                                                              |
| Low-voltage ride-through                      | Survive 0% voltage for 300 ms; linear recovery to 80% within 1.3 s                                                                                                 |
| High-voltage ride-through                     | Up to 120% of nominal for 1 s                                                                                                                                      |
| Post-fault active-power recovery              | \>90% of pre-fault power within 4 s                                                                                                                                |
| Reactive capability                           | Q = ±0.33 pu of rated active power when P \> 20%; ±0.05 pu below 20%                                                                                               |
| Voltage control                               | ±0.5% steady-state accuracy; connection voltages 110/115/132/230/380 kV at ±5% (±10% for 30 min)                                                                   |
| Dispatch set-points                           | Accuracy 2% of set-point or 0.5% of rating; reached within 10 minutes of instruction                                                                               |
| Curtailment modes from the TSP                | Absolute limit, delta (reserve) regulation, and gradient (ramp-rate) limitation                                                                                    |

Idea 2 · Dust-aware derating and cleaning dispatch

The most Saudi-specific idea on the list and, as far as the literature shows, unoccupied at utility scale. Wire the National Center for Meteorology's 72-hour sand-and-dust forecast (dust.ncm.gov.sa) into a soiling model calibrated with published Saudi loss rates (2–50% depending on exposure; about 6% after five weeks in Dhahran and 11.5% after 72 hours in Riyadh; 20% after a single storm). Output: predicted MW loss per block for the next three days, an optimal cleaning-crew route (the 365-block layout is already in your 3D model), and an advance deficit warning to the grid operator. Data risk: NCM has no documented API, so budget a day for scraping or a data request.

Idea 3 · Grid-Code Copilot

A retrieval-augmented assistant over the Saudi Arabian Grid Code, Distribution Code, Generation Expansion Planning Criteria and Transmission Planning Criteria (all public PDFs on SERA) that answers a developer's question ("what reactive capability must a 300 MW PPM provide at 132 kV?") with the clause, and then runs the corresponding test in a simulator. Lower novelty than the twin, but very high deployability — every NREP bidder needs this — and cheap to build. Works well as a feature inside Idea 1.

Idea 4 · Ramp Radar

Aggregate the whole Saudi PV fleet (project locations from the GASTAT renewable statistics and SPPC announcements), combine satellite cloud motion and dust forecasts with RRAtlas station data, and forecast fleet-wide ramps and the reserve the TSO needs hour by hour. This speaks directly to "grid stability with variable renewable production" and is what National Grid SA will need at 50% renewables. Harder to demo credibly in four weeks because you need multi-site irradiance; a version with three or four stations is feasible.

Idea 5 · Surplus-to-Value

For Track 3 · Challenge 2: an orchestration layer that sends midday PV surplus to loads that can absorb it — desalination pumping and storage, district-cooling ice storage, EV fleet charging, and electrolysers — with a tariff simulator. The card's own list of shortcomings (investment, incentives, cyber-risk) is the outline of your differentiation. Strong idea, but it belongs to a different team profile than yours.

Idea 6 · Outage-risk heatmap

The Track 3 · Challenge 1 card hands you SAIDI, SAIFI, line lengths and automation percentage by year; add weather (heat, dust storms) and regional demand growth and produce a regional outage-risk model with a recommendation engine for automation investment. Data-rich and easy to demo, but it is mostly analytics on annual data, so the novelty is modest.

Idea 8 · Cyber-physical anomaly detection — the SAIF angle

For a Ministry of Interior jury, the twin's most interesting property is that it knows what the plant should physically be doing. When SCADA reports something the physics says is impossible — inverter output that ignores the irradiance the weather stations measured, set-points that changed with no operator action — the residual between model and measurement flags a possible intrusion rather than a fault. Position it under "Cybersecurity & Defensive Technologies" or "Sustainability, Energy & Environment", cite the Track 3 card's own line that connected-load infrastructure "increases the risk of cyber-attacks", and demo with injected false-data events. This reuses 90% of the KAPSARC build.
